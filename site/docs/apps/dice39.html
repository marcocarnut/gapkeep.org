dice39 on GapKeep

dice39 turns physical dice rolls into a BIP-39 seed phrase: you supply most of the words with dice and a coin, the device supplies the rest and the checksum, and you can see that every word you typed was kept verbatim.

vendor/ is dice39's own code, copied byte for byte from bip39_generator.html by tools/extract_dice39.py. Each file names the source and its SHA-256. To update, with a checkout of dice39 in ../dice39, run:

python3 tools/extract_dice39.py ../dice39/bip39_generator.html apps/dice39/vendor
git diff apps/dice39/vendor

Phrases of 24 (recommended), 18, 15 or 12 words, as on the page.

lib/selftest.js checks it against the BIP-39, BIP-84 and BIP-93 test vectors at every start, and under Node (node tests/dice39/run_node.mjs). On the Pi Zero it takes about two seconds, so it runs after the start page is up, in steps that leave the screen responsive; Enter my dice words and I already have a seed phrase stay disabled until it has passed.

The launcher icon is the favicon of bip39_generator.html, converted with python3 tools/make_icon.py favicon.png --manifest apps/dice39/manifest.json. lib/cryptoshim.js gives the browser code the two WebCrypto calls it uses, backed by gk:crypto. main.js is the LVGL interface.

Device entropy works as in dice39: the device words come from SHA256(32 random bytes ‖ entropy pad samples), and Generate stays disabled until the entropy pad's bar is full (128 bits at 24 words, 96 at 18 and 15, 64 at 12). The accounting constants are dice39's own (vendor/dice.js); the rules are in lib/entropypad.js. A mouse press scores 4 bits and a drag point 1, and only drags that really moved count. Two additions:

  • Without a mouse, any key typed on the focused pad (Space and Enter too; not Tab, Escape, the arrows or other navigation keys) is a press without a position and scores 3 bits, the clock part of a tap. Holding a key down counts once.
  • The timing of every key pressed in the app is hashed in after the pad samples. It is not credited on the bar.

The 32 random bytes come from GapKeep's hardware random generator (gk:crypto, health-checked and SHA-256 conditioned) instead of crypto.getRandomValues.

A phrase you already have ("I already have a seed phrase"; the page's import mode, a start of its own here): all the words typed, checked by dice39's importMnemonic, which refuses a bad checksum (shown as a likely typo) and never corrects it. Nothing is generated and there is no entropy pad: this splits an existing key. dice39's warning for a phrase in wordlist order or of one repeated word (a published example) is shown; the dice warnings are not, as no dice were thrown.

The result page shows the words and the warnings. That every typed word came back unchanged is still checked, but only a failure is shown (in red); the page's table of each typed word against the output is gone.

The account's public key (not on dice39's page): the BIP-84 native segwit account m/84'/0'/N' on mainnet, as an output descriptor (wpkh([fingerprint/84h/0h/Nh]xpub.../<0;1>/*)#checksum) or a zpub (for Electrum), one QR code at a time with its text (a dropdown picks which; the descriptor first), and the master fingerprint. Account 0 and no passphrase at first; the account number and the BIP-39 passphrase can be changed, and Enter derives the key again (PBKDF2 of 2048 rounds: about 0.1 s on a Pi Zero, with "Working" on screen first). lib/pubkey.js does it with bip39.js, bip32.js and descriptor.js (and crypto.js, psbt/bytes.js, psbt/basen.js, psbt/hashes.js), copies from lib/js/bitcoin/ made by tools/share_libs.py: never edit them here. The log has the keys' lengths, never the keys or the fingerprint.

codex32 shares: a share's QR code is shown directly, with one warning line on its page (a share alone reveals nothing). The split stays while the phrase does, so going back from a share's code shows the same shares. The QR codes of the whole seed (SeedQR, Compact SeedQR, words) still ask first.

Not ported: printing the dice tables (print them from the web page on any computer; they hold no secret), the clipboard export, the network probe (there is no network), and the page's language button: languages are GapKeep's (language in gapkeep.txt, or the Settings app). The Brazilian Portuguese catalog (lang/pt-BR.json) follows the page's own Portuguese where the text is the same.
