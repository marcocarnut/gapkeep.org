E-paper panels

An e-paper panel keeps its picture without power, for years. Wired to the Pi Zero's GPIO header, it lets an app write something a person must keep, a secret share above all, onto a thing that is then unplugged and put away: the share never goes near a phone's camera or a printer, and each panel holds one share. Apps draw on the panel with the same gk:ui widgets as on the screen (API.md, ui.epaper); the kernel turns that into the panel's black, white and red pixels, and drives the panel.

Only the kernel talks to the panel, through a driver for the panels below, and only apps with the "epaper" permission may ask it to. There is no general GPIO or SPI access for apps: on an air-gapped machine that would be a way out for data (a blinking pin, a radio made of a wire).

#Panels

epaper = Module Controller Pixels Colours Refresh
weact-2.9-bwr WeAct 2.9" B/W/R SSD1680 296 × 128 black, white, red about 18 s
weact-2.13-bw WeAct 2.13" B/W SSD1680 250 × 122 black, white about 3 s
weact-4.2-bw WeAct 4.2" B/W SSD1683 400 × 300 black, white about 3 s

(Pixels as an app gets them by default, landscape; portrait turns them.) The 2.9" B/W/R is the one GapKeep is developed with. The driver (src/runtime/epd.cpp) comes from EPDPages' firmware (the same author's e-paper project), where it is proven on these three modules; the 4.2" here has not yet been tried on the device. Other panels of the SSD1680 family are a table row; other controllers (a UC8176, a 384 × 640 B/W/R panel) a few functions (below).

#Wiring

The Waveshare convention, which is also GapKeep's default: SPI0 with its first chip select, and three more GPIOs. The WeAct modules' 8-pin header (J2), its jumper (BS) on "4-line SPI":

Module (J2) Pi Zero header GPIO
1 BUSY pin 18 GPIO 24
2 RES pin 11 GPIO 17
3 D/C pin 22 GPIO 25
4 CS pin 24 GPIO 8 (SPI0 CE0)
5 SCL pin 23 GPIO 11 (SPI0 SCLK)
6 SDA pin 19 GPIO 10 (SPI0 MOSI)
7 GND pin 20 (or 25, 9)
8 VCC pin 17 (or 1): 3.3 V, never 5 V

Pins 17 to 24 sit together on the header (pin 11 is a little apart), so a 2 × 4 or 2 × 5 connector on the Zero's side holds all but RES. None of these are the test rig's (GND on pin 6, GPIO 14 and 15, the RUN pad, 5 V: RIG.md).

Plugging in and out while GapKeep runs is the normal way of working (one panel per share), on a live 3.3 V rail. GapKeep drives none of the panel's lines between refreshes: SPI0's pins and DC, RST and BUSY are all inputs then, so a panel half plugged in gets no current through its signal pins into an unpowered controller. Still:

  • Connect GND first and take it away last: a connector whose ground pin is longer than the others (or a separate ground wire, clipped on first), or plug in the ground row of a 2-row connector before the rest.
  • Series resistors of 100 to 330 Ω in SCL, SDA, CS, D/C and RES, at the Zero's side, limit the current through a pin's protection diodes if a line touches before power does, and calm the edges on long wires. At 4 MHz they don't matter to the signal. BUSY needs none.
  • Plugging in a module charges its capacitors from the 3.3 V rail: a short dip, which a Zero with a good supply rides through. If it ever resets the Zero, add 10 to 47 µF across 3.3 V and GND at the connector, or power the panel through a switch (a P-MOSFET a GPIO turns on) so that it is only powered during a refresh: that is a later improvement.
  • Never unplug a panel while it refreshes (the app says when it may be unplugged): a refresh cut short leaves the panel's pigments part way.

#Text on a panel

A panel's pixels are black, white or red, nothing between: anti-aliased text loses its grey edges, which leaves the smaller sizes thin and ragged. gk:ui's pixel fonts are drawn for it: family: "epdmono", one bit a pixel, each size a design of its own (ui.epdmonoFonts): 10 is X11's misc-fixed 6x10 (public domain, EPDPages' font), 14, 16, 20, 24 and 32 are Terminus Font's bold (8x14 to 16x32, SIL Open Font License, src/runtime/fonts/LICENCE.terminus). Basic Latin and Latin-1, so the codex32 and bech32 alphabets; 0 (slashed) and O, 1, l and I, 5 and S, 8 and B differ.

new ui.Label(panel, { text: "MS12 NAME A320", family: "epdmono", font: 20 });

On the 2.9" (296 x 128) a codex32 share in groups of four fits beside its QR code at 20 (three groups, 14 characters, 140 pixels, a line): dice39 writes its shares so, one panel each, and both dice39 and codex32 the seed's QR codes with the numbered words (Write to e-paper, and Panel only to keep the secret off the screen: apps/dice39/README.md). Their layout (apps/dice39/lib/epaperlayout.js) picks landscape or portrait and the sizes for each panel: the QR code's modules at 3 px where they fit (2 at least), then the largest epdmono text; a 24-word share takes 16 on the 2.9" and the 2.13", 32 on the 4.2". Chosen on the rig's panel, photographed: Terminus bold, two-pixel strokes from 20 up, reads clearly at every size; Terminus regular (one-pixel strokes) came out faint, misc-fixed's 9x18 too; the 6x10 doubled and tripled (the first epdmono 20 and 30) read, but blocky. The anti-aliased fonts work too, from 14 up (mono: true at 16 reads well), at any size of ui.fontRange; epdmono, a bitmap font, has only its own sizes, never drawn from outlines. tools/make_epdmono.py makes the fonts from the PCF and PSF files, whose SHA-256s it checks: the six sizes add about 40 KB to the kernel.

#gapkeep.txt

The card's gapkeep.txt (read at boot, ARCHITECTURE.md) says which panel is wired, and where; without epaper there is none.

epaper = weact-2.9-bwr     # the model, or none
epaper_cs = ce0            # SPI0's chip select: ce0 (GPIO 8) or ce1 (GPIO 7)
epaper_dc = 25             # GPIOs: 2 to 27, not 7 to 11 (SPI0) nor 14, 15 (serial)
epaper_rst = 17
epaper_busy = 24
epaper_spi_khz = 4000      # the SPI clock: 100 to 20000 kHz
epaper_rotate = 0          # how it is mounted: 0, 90, 180 or 270

The values shown are the defaults: only epaper is needed for the wiring above. What is refused is logged (gapkeep.txt:12: epaper_dc: a GPIO number expected...), and a panel whose DC, RST and BUSY aren't three different GPIOs is dropped. The boot log says what was configured: epaper: WeAct 2.9" B/W/R (128x296, red) on SPI0 CE0 (GPIO 8), DC GPIO 25, RST GPIO 17, BUSY GPIO 24, 4000 kHz, mounted turned 0 degrees clockwise. Sealed kernels read no card: their panel is built in (below).

#Mounting: epaper_rotate

How the panel is mounted is the installation's, not an app's: apps ask for landscape or portrait as the person looking at the panel sees it, and GapKeep turns their picture to the panel as mounted. epaper_rotate is the panel's turn from its native position, clockwise, as seen from its front (by the person reading it). Native is the controller's: its first row (gate 0) at the top. The WeAct 2.9" and 2.13" stand taller than wide then; on the 2.9" the first row is at the end with the 8-pin header (J2), the panel's flat cable at the other (as the rig's camera shows it). The 4.2" is wider than tall.

 epaper_rotate = 0        90             180          270
   +--------+     +--------------+    +--------+    +--------------+
   |  TOP   |     |            T |    |        |    | P            |
   |        |     |            O |    |        |    | O            |
   |        |     |            P |    |        |    | T            |
   |        |     +--------------+    |  POT   |    +--------------+
   +--------+                         +--------+
   (a 2.9": its native top row, "TOP", where the viewer sees it)

So 90 hangs a 2.9" wider than tall, its native top on the right; 180 stands it upside down. Then:

  • ui.epaper.info() and panel.width, panel.height are as mounted: landscape is wider than tall for the viewer.
  • An app's page is upright for the viewer when its orientation is the panel's as mounted (landscape on a panel mounted wider than tall). Otherwise it is turned a quarter turn counter-clockwise (the viewer turns the panel a quarter turn clockwise to read it, as a 2.9" mounted at 0 is read in landscape). flip: true adds a half turn.
  • tests/epaper_rotate.txt checks the 16 cases in the simulator, whose pictures show the panel as its viewer sees it, mounted.

#Sealed kernels

A sealed kernel (SEALED=1, ARCHITECTURE.md) reads no card, so its panel is part of the build, with the same keys in capitals:

tools/build.sh pi SEALED=1 EPAPER=weact-2.9-bwr                     # Waveshare's pins
tools/build.sh pi SEALED=1 EPAPER=weact-2.9-bwr EPAPER_BUSY=23 EPAPER_CS=ce1

(EPAPER_CS, EPAPER_DC, EPAPER_RST, EPAPER_BUSY, EPAPER_SPI_KHZ, EPAPER_ROTATE.) The build checks them with gapkeep.txt's rules (tools/epaper_config.py) and stops on a bad one, saying why (EPAPER: EPAPER_DC=9: a GPIO number expected...). They are compiled into the kernel, so the kernel's hash covers them, as it covers built-in apps and data: two sealed kernels for different wiring have different hashes. At boot the log says epaper: the panel is built in (EPAPER=weact-2.9-bwr) and the configuration line above. EPAPER is refused for other builds: they read gapkeep.txt.

Were a kernel to have both (the simulator can: make -C sim EPAPER=..., tests/epaper_builtin.txt), the built-in panel wins and the card's epaper keys are ignored, each with a warning in the log; a sealed kernel has no card, so only its own configuration ever applies.

The keys have the device's name as their prefix so that more SPI devices can come, each on its own chip select (a second panel, an SPI screen): epaper2, lcd... with the same _cs, _dc keys.

#What a refresh does

panel.refresh() takes the picture the panel's widgets make, turns it into the panel's planes (1 bit, and red), in the panel's own orientation, and runs as a long operation: in slices of the main loop, the screen and keyboard alive.

  1. A hardware reset, always (RST low, then high): the panel may be another one than last time, and one in deep sleep hears nothing else. GapKeep never counts on a panel keeping any state.
  2. Then BUSY must go low within a second. BUSY is pulled up at the Zero, so without a panel it stays high: the promise rejects with "no panel answers: is one plugged in?". A soft reset (0x12), the same wait, then the controller's set-up (WeAct's sequence, from EPDPages): the gate lines, the RAM's addressing, the border, the temperature sensor on B/W panels.
  3. Both planes, about 9.5 KB on the 2.9" (19 ms at 4 MHz).
  4. A full refresh with the panel's own waveform, from its OTP memory: the maker's DC-balanced waveform, the one meant for a picture that must last. No partial refresh, no fast or custom waveform: the panel flashes, shows the picture, and on the B/W/R the red comes last (about 18 s). BUSY must go high, and then low within 40 s (20 s for B/W); otherwise the refresh failed ("the panel did not finish its refresh: was it unplugged?"), and a BUSY that never goes high is no panel either.
  5. Deep sleep, then the lines are let go. Only then does the promise resolve: the app can say "you can unplug the panel now".

Nothing is sent to a panel unless an app asked: opening a panel, or drawing on it, sends nothing; boot sends nothing. When the app stops during a refresh, the job is dropped (its planes wiped); the panel finishes the waveform on its own, and the next refresh resets it anyway.

QEMU has no SPI controller: there, ui.epaper.info() is null and ui.epaper.open() throws, whatever gapkeep.txt says (src/spiprobe.cpp looks for SPI0 before anything uses it, since Circle's SPI driver would wait for QEMU's forever).

What the panel shows is never logged, and the buffers that held it (the off-screen display's, the planes) are wiped when freed.

#Keeping a panel

E-paper holds its picture with no power, as pigment particles that stay where they were driven. But no maker promises that for decades, and the pigments age: heat, light (UV), humidity and pressure speed it up, and a red pigment is the one to watch first. For a share kept on a panel:

  • Keep it dark, cool and dry: in an envelope or a box, away from sunlight and heat (well under 30 °C; never a car or a window sill), in a bag with a desiccant where it is damp. Don't bend it, and don't press on the glass.
  • Check it once a year, by looking at it: no power is needed. If it has faded or blurred where you can read the text and scan the QR code only with effort, write it again on a fresh panel, while you still can.
  • For decades, copy the share to metal (stamped or engraved steel plates, as for seed words): the panel is the convenient copy, metal the lasting one.
  • A panel that held a secret holds it still: store it as you would the share on paper, and wipe it (write a blank picture, a few times) before giving it away. Don't reuse a panel for another person's secret.

#Trying it without a panel: the simulator

The simulator has the panels, at the other end of its SPI and GPIO stand-ins (sim/include/circle/spimaster.h, gpiopin.h): the same driver sends its bytes to EPDPages' model of the SSD1680 (sim/src/epd/), which keeps its RAM, plays the refresh and holds BUSY as the real controller does, and ignores everything after deep sleep until a reset, so a driver that forgot the reset shows up in the tests. The 2.9" B/W/R has its pigments' physics too (sim/src/epd/epbwr.c, fitted to film of the real panel): the flashing, the red coming up.

  • tools/gksim --epaper weact-2.9-bwr apps/myapp configures the panel (over the card's gapkeep.txt), with panel "1" plugged in; --epaper-ms 1500 makes a refresh hold BUSY 1.5 s instead of the waveform's 18 s.
  • Scenarios (tools/qemu_harness.py --sim): epaper plug <name> [<model>] plugs in a panel by name (a new one is white; one plugged in before still shows what it showed), epaper unplug, epaper shot <name> saves what the plugged-in panel shows as <name>.png (landscape, in the module's colours, with an 8-pixel white margin for QR readers), epaper refresh-ms <ms>. tests/epaper.txt writes two shares on two panels and checks the pictures (tests/epaper/check_panel.py decodes their QR codes with zbarimg).
  • The browser IDE's E-paper bar: pick a model (the IDE's card then says so in gapkeep.txt), and the panel shows under the screen as its pigments look, the refresh as it happens; Unplug, Plug in and New panel as above.

#Adding a panel

A model of the SSD1680 family (another size, B/W or B/W/R): a row in Models[] in src/runtime/epd.cpp (EPDPages' panels[] has the numbers: size, the RAM X start, the landscape rotation), and in the simulator sim/src/epd/panels.c and a panel_model_t in panel_ssd1680.c. Another controller (the UC8176 of the Waveshare 4.2", a 384 × 640 B/W/R panel's): its init sequence, RAM commands and BUSY polarity in CGkEPD (a family, as GkEPDSSD1683 is), and its model from EPDPages' sim/panel_*.c. Keep the full refresh with the panel's OTP waveform, the reset before and deep sleep after.

#Checking it on the device

The rig's Zero has a WeAct 2.9" B/W/R wired as above, and its Pi Camera looks at it. tests/epaper_hw.txt (rig only) configures the panel over the serial line (#epaper weact-2.9-bwr, test builds: the rig's card never changes), starts tests/apps/epapertest and drives it with #launch and #key (no USB keyboard needed), writes two shares in turn and has the app read each one back with the camera (ui.Scanner, the whole field: the log says which share it saw, never what it says), then a page of text sizes, photographed with serialraw (tools/rig/raw2png.py turns the raw frame into a PNG).

The rig's panel is mounted turned 180 degrees (#epaper weact-2.9-bwr rotate=180): its native top, the J2 end, is at the top of the camera's picture, so a page reads upright for someone at the panel's flat-cable end, the bottom of the camera's picture (tests/epaper_rotate_hw.txt photographs a portrait and a landscape page, and one as if mounted at 90, then writes the landscape page again, so the panel is left upright). tests/epaper_shares_hw.txt has dice39 write two codex32 shares of a test phrase on it, each read back with the camera by the app.

Measured on the rig (2026-11-12), the same at each of three refreshes:
BUSY after the hardware reset already low when first read (10 ms after RST went high)
BUSY after the soft reset (0x12) low when first read, 5 ms after (never seen high)
Both planes sent (9.5 KB at 4 MHz) 19.1 ms, in one slice
BUSY high after 0x20 (refresh) 3.2 ms
The waveform (BUSY high) 18.40 s
From the reset to deep sleep 18.45 to 18.52 s
The job's longest slice 21 ms
The longest gap of a 5 ms JavaScript timer during a write 27 ms (98 ms when the camera was being stopped as the write began)
The job's own time (slices) during a refresh under 100 ms since BUSY is read every 5 ms (it was 2.6 s, in 4 million slices, when it was read on every pass of the main loop)

The QR codes were read back from the panel by GapKeep's own scanner within 2 s, the second write's (another share) as the second share. In the camera's picture the text at 16 and 20 px (monospaced) reads clearly, 14 px reads, 12 px barely, but there the camera is the limit: the panel spans about 200 of its pixels for 296 of the panel's.
