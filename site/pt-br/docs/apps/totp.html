Authenticator

Time-based one-time codes (TOTP, RFC 6238), the six- or eight-digit codes that Google Authenticator, Aegis and the like show for two-factor sign-in, on an air-gapped Pi Zero.

  • Add an account by showing its QR code to the camera ("Scan QR code"), or by typing its key ("Type a key"): Base32, with an issuer and an account name, and the algorithm, digits and period if the service uses other than the usual SHA1, 6 and 30 s.
  • Read the codes: each account shows its issuer and name, the current code grouped for reading (123 456, 1234 5678), and the seconds left until the next one. Enter on an account shows its settings and a key fingerprint, and can remove it.
  • Forget all clears every account.

Everything works from the keyboard: Tab between the buttons and the accounts, Enter to use them, Escape to leave the scanner or the form.

#The clock

TOTP codes are computed from the time (UTC: the time zone doesn't matter). A Pi Zero has no battery-backed clock: after each boot the time must be set, in the Clock app. Until it is, the Authenticator shows no codes (they would be wrong) and says so. It shows the time it uses (UTC) at the top right: compare it with a watch. A clock that is more than a few seconds off gives codes that services refuse near the end of each period.

#Formats

otpauth://totp/LABEL?secret=..., Google's Key Uri Format, what sign-up pages show:

Part
LABEL Issuer:account or account, %-encoded; spaces after the colon are ignored.
secret The key, Base32 (RFC 4648): case-insensitive, padding optional, spaces ignored. Required.
issuer Wins over the label's prefix when both are given. + is a space here (as Android reads it), but a + in the label stays a plus (alice+work@example.com).
algorithm SHA1 (default), SHA256 or SHA512.
digits 6 (default) or 8.
period Seconds, 1 to 86400 (default 30).

Other parameters (image, ...) are ignored. Refused, with the reason on screen: otpauth://hotp/ (counter-based codes), a missing or broken secret, other algorithms or digit counts, a parameter given twice, broken %-escapes.

otpauth-migration://offline?data=..., Google Authenticator's "Transfer accounts" export: Base64 of a protocol buffer holding several accounts, split over several QR codes when there are many. Show the parts in any order: the app says which are still missing, and skips accounts it has already. The export has no period (always 30 s); HOTP and MD5 accounts in it are skipped, with a note. Google doesn't document the format; the field numbers are those of the reverse-engineered migration.proto of dim13/otpauth, which the other open decoders agree with. The decoder (lib/parse.js) is written by hand, with every length checked against the data.

Typed keys must be at least 16 Base32 characters (80 bits): fewer is almost always a key typed in part. A length no whole number of bytes gives (1, 3 or 6 characters past a multiple of 8) is refused as a typo; bits left over after the last whole byte are ignored, as other authenticators do.

#Security notes

  • Nothing is written to the card. GapKeep never writes to the SD card. The accounts are kept in RAM, in the app's session store (gk:session): they are still there when you come back after using other apps, and gone when you switch off. No other app can read them. Your QR codes (or keys) are the backup: keep them, on paper, somewhere safe. Anyone who has them can make your codes.
  • Each account's key is kept (in the app, and in the session store, to outlive the app), with a fingerprint (SHA-256 of the settings and key, cut to 64 bits, shown in the account's details) to recognise the same key scanned twice. Remove and Forget all overwrite the key, in the app and in the session store; switching off forgets everything.
  • The log never holds a key or a code. It goes to the serial port in test builds. For the tests, each code is logged as a tag: the first 64 bits of an HMAC of the code under the account's own key, which only someone holding the key can check (a plain hash of a 6-digit code would give the code away: there are only a million to try). Messages about refused codes never quote the key.
  • The camera's picture never reaches the app: it gets the QR code's text only (CAMERA.md).
  • Codes are on screen while the app is open: leave it when you are done.

#How it works

  • lib/hotp.js: HOTP (RFC 4226) and TOTP (RFC 6238), on gk:crypto's HMAC (native: SHA-1, SHA-256 and SHA-512 in the kernel).
  • lib/parse.js: Base32, Base64, UTF-8, the two URI formats and the protocol buffer.
  • lib/selftest.js: the RFC 4226 HOTP vectors, the RFC 6238 TOTP vectors for all three hashes, HMAC with keys longer than a block (RFC 2202, RFC 4231), the RFC 4648 Base32 vectors, and the parsers on good and bad input. It runs at start, after the first screen is up, in small steps, and logs totp selftest: N passed, 0 failed; if anything fails, no codes are shown. Under Node, with Node's HMAC: node --experimental-default-type=module tests/totp/run_node.mjs.
  • main.js: the screens, and the accounts saved to the session store after each change (restored at start). gk:clock's every(1, ...) ticks on each second: it moves the countdowns, and computes an account's code only when its period turns; when the clock is set or changed, every code is recomputed at once.

Tests: tests/totp.txt (simulator, test build), with tests/apps/clockset setting the clock to an instant where RFC 6238's vectors apply; tests/totp/make_migration.py makes the export QR codes.
