codex32

Recombines codex32 (BIP-93) shares into the BIP-39 phrase they were split from, on an air-gapped Pi Zero. A port of dice39's recombiner page, codex32_recover.html: dice39 (apps/dice39) splits a phrase into k-of-n shares; this app takes any k of them back.

  • Type the shares, one per box, or scan them (each share's QR code, as dice39 prints it). Case does not matter; spaces are ignored, so shares can be typed in groups of four as they are written. There are as many boxes as the first valid share's threshold asks for.
  • Each share is checked as it is typed: the bech32 alphabet, the lengths BIP-93 allows (48, 54, 61, 67, 74 or 127 characters), the threshold, and the checksum. When the checksum fails because of one mistyped character, the app says which character and what it should probably be (BIP-93 recommends suggesting this; the app never applies it itself: you check it against the paper and retype it). The same for one character codex32 does not have (B, I, O, or 1 after the prefix). Shares of another split, or the same share twice, are flagged on the box.
  • Recover the phrase shows what the page shows: the threshold, identifier and number of shares, the 12, 15, 18 or 24 words, the entropy in hex and the codex32 secret (index S), so it can be checked by hand.
  • Getting the phrase off: as on the page, a QR code of the words (one, or three parts), SeedQR or Compact SeedQR, each behind its warning and a confirmation. One is on screen at a time; leaving the page removes it.
  • Clear erases the shares and the result.

Everything works from the keyboard: Enter moves to the next box and, in the last one, recovers; Tab reaches the buttons; Space ticks the confirmations; Escape leaves the camera, the QR pages and About.

#Differences from the web page

  • No clipboard (GapKeep has none), so no copy button.
  • Languages are GapKeep's (language in gapkeep.txt, or the Settings app), not a button on the page. The Brazilian Portuguese catalog (lang/pt-BR.json) has the page's own Portuguese for its strings.
  • One box per share instead of one text area, with the checks above as you type. The page only says "checksum failed".
  • A single string with index S is accepted: an unshared secret (threshold 0, like BIP-93's test vector 1), or a split's secret typed on its own. The page asks for at least two lines, and its cxRecover fails on such a string anyway (below).
  • As on the page, only 16-, 20-, 24- and 32-byte secrets (12, 15, 18 and 24 words) are recovered: dice39 makes no other sizes, and other valid BIP-93 secrets (28 or 64 bytes) are refused with the page's message rather than shown as words they are not.

#What the seed is

BIP-93 encodes a BIP-32 master seed. dice39 puts BIP-39 entropy in the shares instead, and this app (like the page) turns it back into the words. The two give different wallets: a wallet handed the entropy as a raw BIP-32 seed opens a different, empty wallet. The words are what to restore from. (This app does not show the master fingerprint or the xpub yet; dice39 and the Signer do. When it does, they come from the words' BIP-39 seed: see showKeys in main.js.)

#For other apps: lib/codex32.js

A pure ES module (no gk:*, no globals, no logging), usable unchanged by another app, e.g. a signer:

import { recoverSeed, recover, checkShare, Codex32Error } from "./lib/codex32.js";

const seed = recoverSeed(["ms12namea320...", "ms12namecacd..."]);   // Uint8Array
// ... use it, then seed.fill(0)
  • recoverSeed(shares): the secret's bytes, or throws a Codex32Error (code, message, and share, position, suggestion where they apply). The bytes mean what the shares' maker put in them (above).
  • recover(shares): the same with the codex32 secret string, identifier, threshold and share indices.
  • checkShare(text): { ok, share } or { ok: false, error }, for checking a share as it is typed; checkShareSet(parsed) for a set.
  • findSubstitution(text): the one character that makes the checksum valid, if there is one.

It needs vendor/codex32.js beside it in ../vendor/.

#Security notes

  • Nothing is kept: the shares and the result are in the app's memory only, never in the session store (gk:session) or on the card. Leaving the app drops them; Clear erases them. The recovered seed is a Uint8Array overwritten with zeros on Clear, on Back, and before a new recovery. The words and the typed text are JavaScript strings, which cannot be overwritten; they are dropped and the garbage collector run.
  • The log never holds a share, the secret, the seed or a word: it goes to the serial port in test builds. It says what happened, with counts and sizes: codex32: recovered a 16-byte seed (12 words) from 2 shares, codex32: box 1 refused: checksum, a fix suggested.
  • The camera's picture never reaches the app, only the QR code's text.

#How it works

  • vendor/: dice39's code, copied byte for byte from codex32_recover.html by tools/extract_codex32.py (with a checkout of dice39 in ../dice39: python3 tools/extract_codex32.py ../dice39/codex32_recover.html apps/codex32/vendor): the codex32 block (codex32.js, the same code as in dice39's generator page), the BIP-39 wordlist, and from the page's own script its strings, entropy to words and the SeedQR digits (recover.js). Each file names the source and its SHA-256. dice39 is AGPLv3 (LICENSE-dice39.txt).
  • lib/codex32.js: checking and recombining shares, on the vendored code. The typo search uses the checksum's linearity over GF(32): the change one substituted symbol needs is computed for every position in one pass, in small numbers rather than BigInt.
  • lib/selftest.js and lib/vectors.js: every BIP-93 test vector (8 valid, with every k-subset of the shares vectors 2 and 3 give, the alternative encodings of vectors 3 and 4, and all 55 invalid strings, each with the error it must give), sets of shares, typo suggestions, and entropy to words. It runs at start, after the first screen, in small steps (about 2 s on a Pi Zero), and logs codex32 selftest: N passed, 0 failed; Recover stays disabled until it has passed.
  • lib/strings.js: the page's English strings with the HTML taken out, and a few replaced where they speak of a browser, translated through gk:i18n. tests/codex32/run_node.mjs checks that the page's are still its own, word for word.
  • lib/messages.js: the errors of lib/codex32.js (English, for logs and Node) worded again through gk:i18n, from their codes and details; in English the same words (checked by tests/codex32/run_node.mjs).
  • lang/pt-BR.json: the Brazilian Portuguese catalog.
  • main.js: the screens.

#dice39's threshold-0 bug

dice39's cxRecover refuses every threshold-0 string (BIP-93 test vector

  1. with "checksum failed": it interpolates even a single share, and with one point the Lagrange weight is zero, so the result is all zeros. The vendored copy is left as it is; lib/codex32.js decodes a single string with index S directly (cxDecode, cxSeedFromData) instead of calling cxRecover. tests/codex32/run_node.mjs says whether the vendored copy still has the bug.

#Tests

  • node --experimental-default-type=module tests/codex32/run_node.mjs: the self-test, every invalid vector through recoverSeed, and every single-character substitution in every valid vector's shares (about 24,000), each of which must be refused and pointed at.
  • tests/codex32.txt (simulator): typing with a typo, a share of another split, grouped shares, the QR exports, a letter codex32 lacks, vector 1, a 64-byte secret, and the camera (not a share, the same share twice, another split, then three shares). tools/run_sim_tests.sh runs it.
